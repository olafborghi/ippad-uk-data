About

The research project behind the data and how this website is funded.

The IP-PAD project

IP-PAD, short for Interdisciplinary Perspectives on the Politics of Adolescence and Democracy, is a Doctoral Network funded by the European Union under the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA) of Horizon Europe. The network starts from the idea that adolescence and early adulthood, roughly the years between 11 and 21, are a formative period for political attitudes. During these years, young people’s cognitive abilities, emotions and social lives change considerably. We ask how these changes shape the way young people process political information, and in turn how they come to engage with politics.

Because no single discipline can answer this question on its own, IP-PAD brings together political science, political and social psychology, developmental neuroscience and affective science. At the same time, the network trains a cohort of doctoral candidates who carry out their research across these fields.

One part of this work is a panel survey of young people in five countries, Austria, Greece, the Netherlands, Poland and the United Kingdom. The surveys share a common set of questions and invite respondents back in later waves, so that political and psychological development can be followed over time and compared between countries. This website presents the data from the United Kingdom, where the surveys are led by the team at Royal Holloway, University of London. The data page describes how the UK surveys were carried out.

Partners

The network is formed by five universities.

  • University of Amsterdam, Netherlands
  • University of Vienna, Austria
  • Panteion University of Social and Political Sciences, Greece
  • Jagiellonian University, Poland
  • Royal Holloway, University of London, United Kingdom

In addition, six organisations take part in the network as associate partners.

  • European Youth Parliament
  • Joint Research Centre of the European Commission
  • Gallup International
  • Counterpoint Global
  • Democracy Next
  • Public First

More about the network and its other work is available at www.ippad.eu.

This website and the combined data

The combined UK data file and this website are a project by Olaf Borghi at Royal Holloway, University of London. The file brings the four UK surveys together in one place, and the website is meant to open the data to readers who would rather not work with the raw file themselves. If you have questions about the data or the website, please write to olaf.borghi@rhul.ac.uk (send an email).
